Saint John U.S.V.I. Four Panel Maps Showing Terrain and Nature
GEOG 455: Cartography and Geovisualization
November 3rd, 2026
Visualization Software Used: ArcGIS
This is a four-panel map of Saint John showing elevation, ocean depth, tree canopy, and seafloor habitat. Elevation comes from the USGS 1-meter DEM, depth from Sentinel-2 satellite-derived bathymetry, canopy from the NLCD Tree Canopy Cover dataset, and habitat from the Allen Coral Atlas. All data was originally downloaded from Google Earth Engine.

To map elevation, I used a terrain color ramp over the DEM. I also created a hillshade layer that sits over the map at a low opacity. A similar process was used with the sea floor depth layer. I also created contour lines. The tree canopy cover layer is a simple yellow-to-green color ramp, where green would be 99% tree canopy cover. For the Benthic Habitats map, I used a Saint John polygon with some internal gradient, as the land was not the focus of this map. I used a color classification based on each unique habitat.

This 4-panel map is designed to illustrate how even one single place can be visualized in so many ways. This is especially relevant when looking at different ecosystems within an area. Saint John is home to a very rich coral ecosystem across most of its shores. In addition to the corals, Saint John is also heavily forested and is partially a national park. The least forested regions of the island are at Cruz Bay on the southwest of the island and the east end of the island. These two towns are notably outside of the national park designation.
Portland Oregon Roads Map
GEOG 455: Cartography and Geovisualization
October 20th, 2026
Visualization Software Used: ArcGIS
This is a map of roads and airports in the portland oregon region. The map uses a terrain and waterbodies layer as its basemap. The map also uses portland oregon roads and airports from Oregon Metro's RLIS dataset (Regional Land Information System).

The map uses four classes of roads, from interstates down to secondary streets. Using symbology classes I emphasised interstates in orange with a higher thickness, then highways in yellow, and primary and secondary roads in different shades of grey. Airport polygons are shown in red with red text labels and a black halo.

From the map it is clear that I-5 and I-205 form a loop around the city and cross the Columbia on separate bridges, with Portland International Airport on the south bank just west of the I-205 bridge. Due to Portlands unique terrain, the streets on the West Hills have to bend to follow the terrain. 
Portland Oregon Terrain Map
GEOG 455: Cartography and Geovisualization
September 21st, 2026
Visualization Software Used: ArcGIS
This is a map of terrain, waterbodies, and streams in Portland Oregon. The map was created using 1 arc-second elevation dataset from USGS, in addition to Portland waterbodies, streams, and the tri-metro area boundary from Oregon Metro's RLIS dataset (Regional Land Information System).

The map uses a terrain coloramp over the resampled terrai with an added hillshade layer. The map also uses contours every 600 feet and includes the region's rivers and lakes. The streams layer also varies stream width depending on the type of stream. Everything outside of the Tri-Metro boundary is muted using an inverted metro area polygon with shading.

Most of the metro sits on the flat floodplain of the Columbia and Willamette rivers, in places only a few feet above sea level. Notably, the Tualatin Mountains, the ridge that runs northwest along the Willamette, and a scatter of buttes on the east side left by the Boring Volcanic Field, create a unique terrain gradient.
